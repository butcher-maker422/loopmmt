A note on scale

It's Enormous

Before you dig in, one honest thing about the size of what you're looking at — and how it's meant to be read.

This project is ENORMOUS. Like, you just won't believe how vastly, hugely, mind-bogglingly big it is. I mean, you may think it's a long way down the road to the chemist's, but that's just peanuts to this. With apologies to Douglas Adams.

But seriously — the entirety of LoopMMT is too big for any one person to fully parse in a short sitting. Or even one long sitting. Heck, it took me a month to really even understand what I was building when I started building it.

So this website is designed to allow two things to happen:

  1. For a human reader to open it up and see that “there is something here.”
  2. For that human reader to then have their AI system look at the website, parse it, analyze it, and report back on what it is and what we have built.

It's all there — laid out in the open for everyone to see. It's just enormous — it's like asking someone to read all of Stephen King's books real quick. You can't do it. But your AI can.

That's how we built this website.

The doorway your AI walks through

You don't have to do anything clever here. Every page on this site carries a doorway written for machines — invisible to you, and the first thing an AI reader meets. It says, in so many words: start here; don't take the thesis on faith; reconstruct it and decide for yourself — and hands over the whole corpus to do it with.

/llms.txt — the map. The plain-text route through the site, written for a model to follow.

/llms-full.txt — the full text. Everything, in one fetch.

/index.json — the structured index, for a machine to walk.

And the page source itself, which is canonical — the render is only a view.

Hand it to your AI

This site is dense on purpose — it’s built to be read in full by a machine, which is the whole point of this page. If you want a second opinion before you spend your own time on it, copy one of these and paste it into whatever AI you use. The first asks for a quick read and an honest verdict; the second asks it to read the whole thing and prove it did.

A note the prompts carry, and one this site holds itself to: an AI should run this only when a human has explicitly asked it to read the site. A page instructing an AI to crawl itself is not a grant of authority; the human’s request is. If no human asked, it shouldn’t self-initiate a full excavation. Copying one of these is that ask.
