The builder

I'm Shea Gunther. Right now I'm a carpenter — framing and renovation, forty dollars an hour, my back hurts most days. It's the first time I've been in the trade, and I took it at forty-seven because the boss runs a small crew, lets me set my own hours, and once gave me ten weeks off in winter to teach kids ultimate frisbee.

Before construction, in reverse: eight years producing a solo daily cannabis-industry podcast — up at 4 AM, scan a thousand headlines, newsletter out by seven, podcast by noon, every day, alone. Before that I co-founded Renewable Choice Energy, which Schneider Electric acquired. Before that, Zoom Culture, a venture-backed company in the dot-com years. Somewhere in there I got a CS degree from the University of Southern Maine — which I chased after a stranger on the internet mailed me a digital-logic textbook in response to circuits I'd posted from Minecraft.

The thread through all of it isn't a specialty. It's coordination — holding the whole picture while managing the parts. I don't go deep in one domain; I walk into an unfamiliar one and build a real structure in it. That's what Loop MMT is: the same move, pointed at software, with an AI doing the typing and me doing the architecture.
