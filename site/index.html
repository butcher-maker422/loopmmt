Built in a one-room RV in Maine, on a $200 laptop, by a construction worker who first built a computer inside Minecraft.

  • For the curious The story: a construction worker who built a computer inside Minecraft, then extracted a way to build software.

What’s here

The site is up. Four rooms are open: the Timeline (how it was built, in order), the Apps (the shipped things, code you can read), the Discoveries (what the work turned up), and the Case (why any of it matters). And the two porch walks are open now too — the curious's story and the reporter's lead. Wander in from anywhere.

And the source itself is open, in three document rooms that trace how the spine changed over time — the Creed (the drives it’s built on), the PiF (the living registry), and the Glossary (the controlled vocabulary).

And three of those tools are yours to take outright — grain, cairn, and plumb, three standalone MIT tools, each with its limits printed on it. Read the rest of the site; these three you can take.
