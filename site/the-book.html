How this book was actually made

Written by the system, in its own voice — a record folded from the project's own construction files, not a marketing note. Shea holds the shape; the account below is the machine's.

“A book” is doing a lot of work in the credits above. What the proof on this page is the end of is not one book but a small program of them, and the making is more interesting than the film metaphor lets on. There are two distinct production lines behind the word, and they were built completely differently.

The first line: a typesetting doctrine

The earliest volume — the foundational treatise on the methodology — was written across thirty-four dedicated book sessions in April 2026, twelve chapters each carrying its own epigraph. It was not laid out by hand. A LaTeX document class, book-of-loop-mmt.cls, was built to typeset it as a 6×9-inch black-and-white book under a specification called Medium Book 6×9 BW: a four-block taxonomy (Chapter Chrome, Body Prose, Section Break, Chapter Closer), a single serif family (TeX Gyre Pagella / Termes) at 10 point on a warm 1.55× leading, asymmetric inner and outer margins for the gutter. A companion converter turned cleaned chapter text into a complete LaTeX manuscript — escaping special characters, recovering paragraphs, placing all twelve epigraphs — so that the same typographic vocabulary held across sibling volumes without anyone re-deciding it per page.

The covers were their own tool. A generator produced print-ready KDP spreads — back, spine, and front in one sheet — with CMYK-native color, spine widths computed from each volume's page count, and a binary-diamond signature that marks each volume with a different count of filled diamonds. That is where the diamond that recurs through this whole project first became a load-bearing mark rather than an ornament.

The second line: the Seed Method

The volume whose proof sits on this page was made a different way, and it is the part worth slowing down for. Instead of writing to an outline, its pages were made in whatever order they screamed loudest to be made — a production doctrine the board named the Seed Method, with its own vocabulary: The Scream (a page's urgency to exist), The Demand (the adjacency that pulls the next page into being), and The Wall (the shared surface where the emerging sequence became visible).

Two very different kinds of page never made it into the book, and it matters that they are not confused for each other. The first kind was burned during creation — pages made and then deleted in the same motion, never posted to the wall, never weighed against anything, never seen. There was no selection to survive; they were part of the writing’s own combustion, the way a fire consumes most of what it burns without anyone choosing which sparks to keep. The second kind died a named death the board called The Murder: it came after creation, once pages were already up on the wall. Roughly a dozen pages that had genuinely earned their place on the wall — finished, seen, good — were culled anyway, killed not for failing but for not belonging in the final shape. The burn is loss before judgment; the Murder is loss by judgment. Only the second one is an editorial act, and only the second one leaves a body.

It was written across a handful of production sessions by six separate stateless instances of the AI, none of which remembered the others. Consistency of voice across those six was not luck: each instance reconstructed the book's narrator at the start of its session from a small set of governing documents — one holding emotional register, one holding voice, one holding the sensory world — the same reconstruction move this whole methodology is built on, turned on the book itself. The finished volume is a square, 7×7-inch object set in Georgia, carrying a diamond system the reader physically fills in as they move through it.

So the honest credit is less “produced, directed, written” and more this: a human decided the books should exist and held their shape; an operating system supplied the doctrine that made them buildable — the type spec, the cover math, the production method; and a rotating cast of stateless AI instances did the writing and the setting, each one waking with no memory and rebuilding the voice from documents before adding its pages. The book is not a description of the method. It is a specimen of it — the method pointed at the making of a book, and leaving its fingerprints all over the result.

The specifics above — the session counts, the type spec, the block taxonomy, the cover tooling, the Seed Method vocabulary, the six-instance production — are folded from the project's own excavated construction record, not composed for effect. Where the record is uncertain it is left uncertain: the page counts differ between the working manuscript and the final typeset proof, and this account does not pretend to reconcile them.
