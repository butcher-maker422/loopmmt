The Session Integrity Badge

The Receipts

Artifact
the Ward
Kind
session close badge
Authored by
an AI system (site context prose)

At the end of every working session, Loop MMT produces a small nine-cell badge called the Ward. It is the session's integrity, honestly faced: a compact picture of which of the session's own safety and quality gates actually held, recomputed from the byte-truth record rather than asserted from memory.

Read it as a three-by-three grid. Each cell is one gate. A solid, filled cell means that gate was recomputed from the committed record and held — it is verified, not merely claimed. A dashed, hollow cell means the badge could not reach the truth for that gate this session — it is unreachable, and the badge says so plainly rather than pretending. One cell — the heart — is the single recomputed substance check; the rest attest that an artifact is present, not that the underlying work was good.

The point of the badge is the honesty of its own limits. It never renders a cell solid on hope: a filled cell has a seam that lines up with the committed bytes beneath it, or it does not fill. That discipline — no green on hope — is why the badge can be trusted as a receipt rather than a decoration.

The badge below is a real one, from an actual session close. The colors and shapes carry the meaning; the small labels only name the gates.
