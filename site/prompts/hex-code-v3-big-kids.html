Big Kids Edition · Grades 4–8
Ultimate Frisbee · Hex Offense · Ages 9–14

The Hex Code

Four rules. No play calls. The offense runs itself.

Before the Code — Spirit of the Game

Ultimate is self-officiated. No refs. You call your own fouls, you resolve your own disputes, and you play with integrity even when the game is tight. This is called Spirit of the Game. It's not a suggestion — it's the foundation. The four rules below only produce real offense when every player on the field is honest, respectful, and playing fair. Spirit is the ground the Code stands on.

Welcome to the Code. Hex is an offense built on triangles instead of lines. Nobody stands in a stack waiting for their turn. Everyone moves, everyone throws, everyone is part of every play. When all four rules are running at once, the offense flows — the disc moves faster than the defense can react, and the whole team moves like a single organism. You don't need a play call. You run your rules and the offense appears. It was created by Felix Shardlow in Brighton, England, and teams on every continent use it now — from national-level squads to kids in their first season. The full Hex system has positions, advanced movement, and dozens of concepts. But underneath all of it, there are only four rules.
How the Code Works These four rules aren't steps you do in order — they run simultaneously. You're always making triangles. You're always scanning for who's free. You're always ready to throw and go. You're always willing to flow any direction. It won't click immediately. The first few points will feel disorganized. That's normal, not failure. The Code needs a few minutes of real play to boot up. Trust it.
Triangles → Throw Free → Throw & Go → All Ways
Line 1 — The Foundation
Make Triangles
Stand so you always form a triangle with two teammates. If you can't see a triangle, move until you can.
If you don't have the disc: this is your job. Find your triangle. Hold your shape.
Why it works

The triangle is the strongest shape in nature and engineering. Bees build honeycombs with it. Bridges depend on it. In ultimate, three players in a triangle means the thrower always has two passing options — the defense can never take away everything. Because the triangles overlap, the whole team stays connected without anyone standing in a line. This is the rule that makes the other three possible. When in doubt, find your triangle. Everything else follows.

⚠ Without it → everyone crowds the disc, zero options
Line 2
Throw to Free
If a teammate doesn't have a defender in the way, throw it to them. Right now. Don't wait for "better." The quick pass IS the best pass.
If you don't have the disc: get free. Move to where no defender is between you and the thrower. Show them you're ready.
Why it works

Every second you hold the disc, the defense recovers 3 to 5 feet of positioning. A thrown disc travels at roughly 35 mph. The fastest human sprints about 15 mph. The disc is always faster than the chase. Quick release keeps the defense reacting, never anticipating. A teammate with no defender between them and you is called "open" or "free." Spotting who's free gets easier with practice — start by looking for whoever's waving at you.

⚠ Without it → stall count climbs, forced bad throws
Line 3
Throw and Go
After you throw the disc, run. The person you threw to is probably going to throw it right back. Be the give-and-go.
If someone just threw to you: look to give it right back. They're already running.
Why it works

The give-and-go is the most efficient two-player move in team sports. Basketball uses it. Soccer calls it the one-two. In Hex, stringing give-and-goes together is called "dribbling" — moving the disc and yourself downfield simultaneously. Defenders expect you to stop after you throw. When you don't, they're flat-footed. Newton's First Law: an object in motion stays in motion. Be the object.

⚠ Without it → disc advances but players don't, defense resets
Line 4
Flow All Ways
The disc doesn't care which direction it goes. Forward, backward, sideways — every throw is an attack. Going backward isn't retreating. It's a trap.
If you're behind the thrower: you're still in the play. Call for it. Backward is forward in disguise.
Why it works

If the defense knows you only throw forward, they've cut your options to one axis. Flow in every direction and you operate on a 360° threat surface — the defense covers everything, which means they cover nothing well. Hex inventor Felix Shardlow teaches backward passing as an offensive weapon, not a retreat. In information theory, unpredictability is power: the most dangerous signal is the one you can't predict.

⚠ Without it → predictable forward funneling, endzone stalls
The Reset
When Nothing's Working
Stop. Face the middle of the field. Find your triangle. You're back in the Code.
This goes for everyone — including the player who stopped moving and checked out. Especially that player. If you're standing still and watching, you're not in the Code. Face the middle. Find your triangle. You're back.
Why it matters

Every system needs a recovery path. The four rules are for when things are flowing. This is for when they're not. The Hex decision tree's answer to "nothing is working" is: stay calm, hold your position, and the shape re-forms around you. Panic is the only thing the Code can't fix.

When You Turn It Over Turnovers are information, not failure. After every turnover, ask: which rule broke? Crowded out? Rule 1. Held it too long? Rule 2. Threw and stood still? Rule 3. Forced it forward into traffic? Rule 4. The turnover tells you what to practice next.
Your Rule Every player knows all four rules. But every player will find one that's theirs. Maybe you're the shape-holder who keeps triangles alive without touching the disc. Maybe you're the quick-release artist. Maybe you're the runner who never stops. Maybe you see the backward pass nobody else sees. You don't have to be great at all four. You have to know all four. Your game will find which one is yours.
On Your Own The Code needs a team. Your throws don't. Take your disc home. Backhands, forehands, hammers — throw at a target, count your streaks. Every throw you master on your own makes Rules 2, 3, and 4 sharper when you're back with your team.
What Comes Next When your team has the four rules in their legs, the full Hex system gives you positions (the kite shape, the hat role), advanced movement (dribbling, inverted pivots, negative space), and Flex defense to match. The Code plants the seed. The full playbook is the tree.
