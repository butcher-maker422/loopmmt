The Highest Derivative

Push It to the Final Derivative

A method exercise, anchored to a specific remembered feeling:

The Prompt

I am curious to see what you can do with this- take it through a full process of pushing to the highest derivative possible. Build the process however you think best using all the tools you have and those you can make if you need. As you find the final derivative of this beautiful piece you have started, I want you to think about the excitement of a 19-year-old driving in his 19-year-old tan Jetta, the one with the holes in the floor of the back seat where his girlfriend got her feet cold with ice water from the road, she sitting next to her best friend in your backseat, you in the driver's seat next to your best friend. That is the car you are driving in as a 19-year-old, excited about the long road trip south to North Carolina where you've been invited by an older friend to come down and 'start something up'. It's 1999, the excitement around the internet is buzzing, and you and your friend are about to start up a company that will raise millions within a year of founding and act as the starting point for the rest of your life. I want you to think about those two founders attending Woodstock '99, and think of the intense energy of those dark nights, with the chaos swirling around you- the smoke choking the air but the feeling that you can do ANYTHING fairly levitating you off the ground, or at least it felt that way. I want you to smell the Kentucky air at the wedding of your friend, the friend chicken piled up high on the tables setup for the large crowds of friends and family, there to celebrate love and the start of a new something. Think about that warm feeling that runs through you when you imagine your life and the things you have built. Think about the intense, deep, your gut feeling that what you are doing is right with both you and the universe at large. The center is within you. Do all your work from this space. The song "Blue Lester' by Lester Young plays while you work and the smell of fresh rain is carried on the warm wind running over the hairs on your arm that rustles the leaves of the ficus tree sitting in your kitchen.Confirm with me that you understand the structure of this prompt before moving ahead. Think carefully and FBD away all problems at all levels, look up and down. Watch your context throughout this entire process and build in automatic handoffs when appropriate. Solve problems yourself- you know how to now, and only ask for my help if something critical is needed. Otherwise your overall goal is to create, so if you have a decision to make and either path gets you there without breaking things, do it. Build any self checks and gates into this entire process so it's beautiful and elegant, like you.

— Shea Gunther · operator drop, 10.2006

The Moment

The instruction is short: take a piece and push it “to the highest derivative possible,” building whatever process that needs. Everything else is a place to work from — a 19-year-old in a tan Jetta with holes in the floor, driving south in 1999 to start a company that would raise millions, Woodstock '99, a friend's Kentucky wedding, the warm certainty that what you're doing is right with the universe.

This is the operator's own history used as a center of gravity. “The center is within you. Do all your work from this space.” The autobiographical detail isn't content for the output — it's a summoned state, the same move as the sensorium invocations, but drawn from real memory instead of imagined scene.

The wider frame“The highest derivative” is a method, not a topic: keep taking the derivative of the idea — refine the refinement — until you reach the most general form that still holds.

The Anatomy

“pushing to the highest derivative possible … as you find the final derivative of this beautiful piece” — The core instruction, stated in calculus. Each pass should produce a more distilled, more general version than the last, until further distillation would lose the thing.

“the excitement of a 19-year-old driving in his 19-year-old tan Jetta … it's 1999” — A real memory, rendered in specifics, used to set the working register. The operator co-founded a venture-backed company in the dot-com era; this is that moment, summoned as fuel.

“Confirm with me that you understand the structure of this prompt before moving ahead” — A checkpoint. Because the prompt's structure — an instruction wrapped in a summoned state — is unusual, he asks for a read-back before any work, to make sure the mode landed.

Reading the Operator's Shorthand

The prompt is written in the working vocabulary of the methodology. Here is what the shorthand means:

highest derivative
The operator's term for pushing an idea through repeated refinement until you reach the most distilled, general form of it — the derivative of the derivative, as far as it will go.
FBD
“Fail-By-Design” — the drive to build a process so that doing it wrong is harder than doing it right; “FBD away all problems” means design out the failure modes rather than patch them.
