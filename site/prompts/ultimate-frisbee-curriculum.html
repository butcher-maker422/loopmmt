Ultimate Frisbee 5-Week Curriculum

Three Tiers, Grades 2–8

A real coaching deliverable, fully specified down to the 45 working minutes:

The Prompt

I need you to design a five week curriculum for my ultimate frisbee league- it's for kids in grades 2-8, where the kids are broken up in grades 2/3, 4/5, and 6/7/8, so the curriculum should be designed in three tiers- with the youngest ones being simpler. Each week the kids meet on Mondays and Wednesdays for one hour. They warm up for the first 15 minutes, so the lesson plan should account for that and block out activities in the last 45 minutes. Not a huge fan of complicated drills, and I focus on teaching the kids how to throw and catch. I teach them the backhand and forehand throws, plus I teach them three angles to throw at - what i call flat, inside, and outside, but that disc golfers call hyzer and anhyzer I think? I also teach them the scoober and forehand and offhand backhand. I also teach the kids hex, which you should look up in KPs- there are a few KPs about ultimate you should look at. Think about math and science and patterns how to teach kids. ~grove+jetta Super RCR to develop the content and then another Super RCR to write the document as BWPDF, to be printed out by me. Do a Super Frame somewhere in there, registering your types, and a few Full Frames. Super AX. Kick me out docs when you are done. Make sure to self review and use all the resources you need to get this done. Again, Super AX.

— Shea Gunther · operator drop, 10.2006

The Moment

This is the methodology pointed at the operator's actual life. He coaches youth ultimate and needs a real curriculum: five weeks, three tiers by grade band (2/3, 4/5, 6/7/8, simplest for the youngest), Mondays and Wednesdays, one hour with the first fifteen minutes for warm-up, so the plan blocks out the last forty-five. His pedagogy is specific — throwing and catching over complicated drills, backhand and forehand, three release angles he calls flat/inside/outside (the disc golfer's hyzer and anhyzer), the scoober, the offhand backhand, and Hex, which the board is told to look up in the ultimate KPs.

The working recipe is named exactly: a discovery-and-jetta sensorium, one Super RCR to develop the content and a second to write it as a BWPDF he prints, a Super Frame with registered types, a few Full Frames, self-review, and Super AX throughout. “Think about math and science and patterns how to teach kids.” It is a production job with a hard, physical deliverable at the end — a document that goes to a field full of second-graders.

The wider frameThe methodology earns its keep when it produces something a nine-year-old uses without ever knowing it existed. A printed lesson plan for a frisbee field is as real a monument as any protocol.

The Anatomy

“three tiers- with the youngest ones being simpler … block out activities in the last 45 minutes” — The hard constraints: grade-banded difficulty and a real time budget. The curriculum has to fit an actual practice, not an idealized one.

“three angles to throw at - what i call flat, inside, and outside, but that disc golfers call hyzer and anhyzer … the scoober … hex, which you should look up in KPs” — The domain content, in the operator's own terms, cross-referenced to the corpus's ultimate KPs — ground the curriculum in what he actually teaches and what the KPs already hold.

“~grove+jetta Super RCR to develop the content and then another Super RCR to write the document as BWPDF … Super AX” — The production pipeline: sensorium, two Super RCRs (develop, then write), a Super Frame and Full Frames, self-review, and autonomous execution — ending in a printable document.

Reading the Operator's Shorthand

The prompt is written in the working vocabulary of the methodology. Here is what the shorthand means:

sensorium
The methodology's cognitive-environment layer: a composed set of sensory channels (a “~discovery+grove” mix, here) that sets the working mood before the thinking starts.
Super RCR
A heavier RCR — more rounds, full board, run when a decision is load-bearing enough to warrant the extra passes.
Super Frame
A wider Full Frame — the complete context plus the surrounding structure it sits in.
Full Frame
A deliberation setting where the board reasons over the complete context rather than a slice — nothing held back.
KP
Knowledge Pack — a self-contained reference document the board can pull in to ground a discussion in a specific domain.
AX
A close-out marker: “execute autonomously” — make the plumbing decisions yourself, only surface the real forks.
Hex
A hexagon-based offensive scheme in ultimate frisbee; the operator coaches a variant of it and wants it reduced to a few generative rules.
