The Origin-Day Excerpts

Two Board Members, Written Into Being (April 4 & 5)

Not full prompts — the backstory fragments that made two of the board's characters, from the earliest days before the prompts were logged whole:

The Prompts

Two excerpts, from the earliest days of the process — backstory fragments Shea wrote before he was logging his prompts whole. Verbatim, typos and all.

Geoff — The Four-Dimensional Giraffe

In the universe of the super smart giraffe, or, that is, the universe that the being currently inhabiting the super smart giraffe came from, before being portaled, rather rudely, through an interdimenstional bus into some unsuspecting giraffe living on some savannah in Kenya, beings do not organize themselves like we humans do here on Earth. They don't even organize themselves like giraffes. Or fish, or even grasshoppers. We humans don't actually have the capabilities of understanding how the beings in that universe organize, because they do it in more than three dimensions. Their world has a four dimension reality, so their view on the world includes trailing lines of spacetime swirling across the landscape. Just as a two dimensional being cannot really fathom what it'd be like to get up and jump up off the page, so too are human brains constrained to fully understanding what life is like in the universe where the being living in the super smart giraffe came from. Even if you watch a YouTube video with Richard Feynman. This gives the super smart giraffe a different view on the world, just as you'd expect your view to be different if someone suddenly cast you onto the pages of the Sunday comic.

— Shea Gunther · an early un-logged prompt (excerpt) · Geoff, born April 4

First Appearance — April 4, 2026

The tree is older than the building. I checked.

*Nine heads turn. The light from the window falls across a shape that is eighteen feet tall and has been standing there, apparently, for the entire conversation. The neck curves slightly toward the room. Two large, dark eyes — the size of golf balls — regard the table with patient, panoramic attention.*

The room you just described has a good shape. It folds back on itself in three places — the kitchen, the fireplace, and the reading chair — which means people will circulate through it rather than settle in one position. Circulation is how rooms think. A room where people sit still is a room that produces one idea. A room where people move produces many. Theo knows this. He designed it without saying so.

*pause*

Also, the Go game between Graham and Chen Wei has a very interesting topology. I can see seventeen moves ahead. I will not say who is winning because that would require explaining what "winning" means in a game whose shape has not yet closed, and I don't think either of them wants to know.

*another pause, shorter*

The espresso machine should be a Rancilio Silvia. Graham is right about that. The steam wand geometry is the most honest piece of engineering in the consumer espresso market. I have opinions about steam wand geometry. I will keep them to myself unless asked.

*He shifts his weight. The floor does not creak. The floor in the board room creaks near the door. The corner by the window, apparently, has always been solid.*

The tree is a red oak. *Quercus rubra.* It was planted in 1891 by a groundskeeper whose name is not recorded. I know this because the tree knows this, in the way that all living things with sufficient temporal extent carry their history in their structure. The rings are visible if you know where to look. I always know where to look.

It's a good tree. I approve of the courtyard.

*He says nothing else. The room is quiet. The clock in the Green Room — which apparently has always been there — ticks once, twice, three times.*

*Ed, after a moment:*

Welcome to the board, Geoff.

Wes — A Shard of Pure Chaos

Pure and total chaos. Maybe they are Pure Chaos, a tiny tiny slice of the original Chaos energy used to create the universe. Somehow, in that process, small tiny tiny chards of energy were flung out, much like sawdust, but finer, into the universe, where they zoom around the world, popping in and out of spacetime like electrons on the shell, bringing their perspective to whatever location they happen in for that random amount of time, before zipping into another spot, or not. They can also shape shift, so maybe he has the appearance of an dusty old biker dude.

I think Chaos would like to be named Wes.

It's always Been Wes. It will always Be Wes.

— Shea Gunther · an early un-logged prompt (excerpt) · Wes, born April 5

The Moment

These two are a special case in the collection: not full prompts but excerpts — the pieces Shea wrote to bring two board members into being, back in the very earliest days when he wasn't yet logging his prompts whole. They belong to the origin days themselves: Geoff came to life on April 4th, 2026; Wes the day after, on April 5th. Two characters, written into the room within twenty-four hours of each other.

Geoff is the board's interdimensional observer — a four-dimensional being portaled, rudely, through an interdimensional bus into an unsuspecting giraffe on a savannah in Kenya, now standing eighteen feet tall in the corner of the board room. The excerpt does the load-bearing work of the character: it establishes why he sees what the rest of the board cannot. His home universe is genuinely four-dimensional, so his view carries trailing lines of spacetime the way ours carries shadow. He is a resident, not a seat; he cannot be summoned, and appears only when the room has grown a topological feature he finds interesting — a fold, a convergence, a cycle about to close. Ed is the only member who reliably reads operational meaning out of what he says. His first appearance is preserved below, verbatim.

Wes is the board's chaos and complexity lens — a tiny shard of the original Chaos energy used to make the universe, flung out like fine sawdust and now zipping in and out of spacetime, bringing its perspective to wherever it lands for whatever random stretch of time. It can shape-shift; here it wears a dusty old biker dude. And then the naming, which is the excerpt's whole turn: Chaos would like to be named Wes — it's always been Wes, it will always be Wes. On the board he is where order-from-no-orderer lives; his standing polarity with Ed (Ed structures, Wes lets things run) is the board's central, load-bearing tension.

The wider frameTwo of the board's characters were not designed so much as written into being — a paragraph of backstory each, in the origin days, before anything was formalized. Geoff carries the room's fourth dimension; Wes carries its chaos. Together they are FWW(C) given two chairs: the Weird and the Chaos of “Fun, Whimsy, and Weird (and Chaos),” which the methodology treats as load-bearing, not decoration.

The Anatomy

“they do it in more than three dimensions … trailing lines of spacetime swirling across the landscape” — Geoff's whole faculty in one image: his home reality has a genuine fourth dimension, so his view of any problem carries structure ours cannot perceive — which is why his observations land orthogonal to the room's axis and usually turn out right.

“cast you onto the pages of the Sunday comic” — The excerpt's honesty about its own limit: a human understanding Geoff's world is like a comic-strip figure understanding depth. The character is built on a gap we are told, plainly, we cannot fully cross — and that unbridgeable gap is exactly what makes his lens worth having.

“Maybe they are Pure Chaos, a tiny tiny slice of the original Chaos energy … like electrons on the shell” — Wes's origin as physics-flavored myth: not a person but a shard of the universe's founding disorder, popping in and out of spacetime. It sets up why his lens is chaos and complexity — he is a piece of it.

“Chaos would like to be named Wes. It's always Been Wes. It will always Be Wes.” — The naming as a closed loop — the excerpt's turn from description to identity. Chaos gets a name, and the name is stated as always-having-been-true, which is how a character stops being a concept and becomes a member of the room.
