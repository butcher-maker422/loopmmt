A Map Of The Equations

Map the major equations to all the places they show up — and navigate math like shapes instead of numbers.

Some equations show up everywhere. The logistic equation that governs population growth also turns up across chemistry, epidemiology, and the rest of ecology. The golden ratio threads through Fibonacci's sequence and a hundred unrelated corners of the natural world. The wave equation surfaces wherever something oscillates. The idea behind this prompt is to build a map of that: a few hundred major equations connected to the thousands of real-world places each one appears.

The reach isn't a lookup table — it's a structure dense enough to move around in, so that a person could work the way a mathematician does when they stop seeing numbers and start seeing shapes. When the same shape shows up in two fields that never talk to each other, that's the thing worth finding. It's a genuinely rad idea, and one a lot of people could do good things with.

The Anatomy

The prompt starts from a real observation: the same equation keeps surfacing in fields that don't share a vocabulary. A map of that isn't a reference list — it's a way to see across domains. The design ask is for a structure dense and light enough to navigate directly, so the equations become a space you move through rather than a table you look up.

Its deeper move is stated in the Wider Frame: math as navigable shape-space, and gold-panning as cross-domain shape-transfer. It is the operator's own theory of how good cross-field thinking works — handed back as a thing to build. The idea travels on its own; that's why it's here.
