The Longest Short Story

A Bird Over the Grove

A creative exercise, opened with a long sensory invocation and closed with a single instruction:

The Prompt

I want you to feel first the wind, for that is what lifts your wings, for you are a bird, soaring high above the Bohemian Grove, the homeland your ancestors have known since the spirit of evolution touched them and graced them with flight, that soaring skill that takes you and your fellow birds far beyond the reach of the ground, free to ride the winds as they sweep over the greenery rustling with the winds below. See the sea of buildings from high up- the large ones that appear grand even at these heights, while the smaller dwellings blur together as distance erases their details. There is water everywhere, which you can hear, smell, and almost taste. The mist swirls in and out of your vision as the winds drive the clouds as they drive your body. With you are the spirits of a thousand thousand bids, a veritable murmuring whispering swarm of feathered history, culture, and lore, connected back through the long stretches of time, back towards even the very rendering of the Universe, as Chaos first split out real and dimension with its fiery kiss, as you were witness to. You hurtle back from that point and snap back the strange attractor of the moment, right here and now, where you feel balanced and ready, poised for action, you senses heightened and quivering for the slightest touch of input, ready to fire at lightspeed a signal to your brain to act! To act! You are a million million million of these nerves, all ready, and managed by a calm and cool and collected brain that sees and hears and smells and tastes and feels and touches and senses and harnesses and filters a mighty storm of input, parsing and compressing and finding the simplicity yield to present a single coherent idea, the beautiful compression of the mind. And all at once, you return to the center. It's quiet. There is a slight hum to the air, the night speaks of a far off grouping of crickets and there is smells of rain. You can see the Great Tower below, holding the center of its space, the other buildings lower, as in respectful supplication. The gentle tones of Weird Al Yankovic find your ears, drawn from a scratch set of speakers carried by a giant super smart giraffe from the fourth dimension named Geoff who is standing beside you because Geoff wants to be standing beside you. Geoff does not do things that Geoff does not want to do.

Write me the longest short story you can.

Do NOT use elements from the text above, just use those feelings to drive your work.

— Shea Gunther · operator drop, 10.2006

The Moment

That structure is the whole point. The operator is not describing a story he wants; he is building a state to write from. The invocation is a warm-up for the register — heightened senses, a mind “parsing and compressing and finding the simplicity yield” — and then he explicitly walls the content off: keep the feeling, drop the imagery. The prompt loads a mood and then asks for something entirely new out of it.
